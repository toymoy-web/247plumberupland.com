Euclid Avenue is the wide boulevard that runs through the middle of Upland, and the City of Upland has designated a historic district along both sides of it, from 8th Street to 24th Street. This page is for homeowners in that corridor who want to know how a plumbing visit works before they call.

We are based in Upland, so this district sits inside our home service area. Our line is answered 24/7, and we explain the options and the price before any work starts.

Verified Facts

What The City Records Say About The District

Boundaries

The City of Upland describes the district as running along both sides of Euclid Avenue between 8th Street and 24th Street.

Designation

The city designated the district on April 28, 2004. The designation describes it as residential and lists 1895 to 1936 as its period of significance.

Registered Status

The city's historic register list shows Euclid Avenue as registered at the national and state level on August 10, 2005.

Our Approach

How We Work Along A Corridor Of Different Homes

Homes along a corridor this long have been built, remodeled and repaired over many decades. Neighboring houses can have very different plumbing, so we do not guess from the street or the year on a listing. We look at what is actually in the walls, under the floor and in the yard.

For a drain that keeps backing up, we start with a camera inspection so you can see the condition of the line before choosing between cleaning, repair and replacement. Our sewer line repair and drain cleaning work both begin from that picture.

Mature landscaping near a house can affect a sewer lateral, but it is only one possible cause. A camera shows whether roots, a sag in the pipe or a damaged joint is behind the problem.

Low pressure, discolored water or a bill that climbs for no clear reason deserves a proper look. Our leak detection service pinpoints where water is going before anyone opens a wall or digs up a yard.

Before You Call

Three Things That Speed Up The Visit

Know Your Main Shutoff

Find the main water valve now. In an emergency, closing it limits damage while you wait for a plumber.

Note What Changed

Tell us when the symptom started and which fixtures are affected. It helps us arrive with the right tools.

Share Past Work

If you know of a repipe, a sewer repair or a water heater change, tell us. We work from facts, not assumptions.

Be Clear On The Limits

Planning Rules And Property Details

Some properties in a designated historic district may face review for exterior changes. We have not verified what applies to any specific address. Before work that changes the outside of a home, such as moving a water heater vent, check with the City of Upland Planning Division. We also do not know the age, pipe material or repair history of any individual home here, so we confirm those on site.

FAQs

Euclid Avenue Historic District Plumbing Questions

Where is the Euclid Avenue Historic District?

According to the City of Upland, it covers both sides of Euclid Avenue between 8th and 24th Streets. If you are not sure whether your address is inside the boundary, call anyway. We can help with the plumbing question either way.

Do you work on homes in the district?

Yes. It is part of our Upland home service area. Tell us what is happening and the age of the house if you know it, and we will plan the visit around the problem.

How do you know what pipe my home has?

We check. Age alone does not tell us, because many homes have been partly or fully repiped over the years. On site we look at exposed lines, read water pressure and, when needed, put a camera in the drain.

Do I need city approval for plumbing work in a historic district?

We have not verified any rule that applies to interior plumbing. Exterior changes may be reviewed, so check with the City of Upland Planning Division first. We explain what permits a job needs before we start.

Do you offer emergency service on Euclid Avenue?

Yes. Our line is answered 24/7. A burst pipe, sewage backup or water heater failure here gets the same dispatch as anywhere else in Upland. Shut off the main water valve if you can reach it safely, then call.
