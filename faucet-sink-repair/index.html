A dripping faucet is usually a worn cartridge, O-ring, or valve seat, an inexpensive fix once we know which part has failed. A leak under the sink is a different issue, usually a loose or corroded supply line connection, and it's worth fixing quickly since it often goes unnoticed until the cabinet floor is already damaged.

We handle repair and full replacement for kitchen and bathroom faucets and sinks, including upgrading to a new fixture if the existing one is outdated or simply not repairable.

The Cause, Explained

Common Faucet And Sink Issues

Upland's hard water accelerates mineral buildup inside faucet cartridges and valves, which is a common cause of drips, reduced flow, and stiff handles that didn't use to be that way.

Under-sink leaks are often at the supply line connections or the P-trap, especially in older homes where original fittings have corroded over the years. These leaks are sometimes slow enough to go unnoticed until a musty smell or soft cabinet floor gives it away.

A faucet that's difficult to turn or has started squeaking usually has the same mineral-buildup cause as a drip, just at an earlier stage before it starts leaking outright.

A pull-down kitchen sprayer that leaks at the base or won't retract properly is usually a hose or docking issue rather than the main faucet body, and it's diagnosed separately from a standard drip.

Signs To Watch For

Signs You Need Faucet Or Sink Repair

Persistent Dripping

Wastes water continuously and usually means a worn internal part.

Low Water Pressure At One Fixture

Often mineral buildup inside the faucet itself, not a house-wide issue.

Water Under The Sink

Check the cabinet floor regularly. A slow leak here often goes unnoticed for a while.

Stiff Or Hard-To-Turn Handles

A sign of internal corrosion or mineral buildup worth addressing before it fails completely.

FAQs

Common Questions

Why does my faucet drip even when fully closed?

Usually a worn cartridge, O-ring, or valve seat inside the faucet body. It's a straightforward, inexpensive repair once identified.

Is a small leak under my sink urgent?

It's worth addressing promptly. Even a slow leak can damage the cabinet floor and create a mold risk over time.

Can you match my existing faucet finish?

In many cases yes, though very old or discontinued finishes sometimes require a full fixture replacement to match well.

Does hard water really affect faucets that much?

Yes, mineral buildup inside the valve and cartridge is one of the most common reasons faucets in Upland start dripping or feel stiff.

How long does a faucet repair usually take?

Most cartridge or valve repairs take under an hour once we've identified the part. A full fixture swap takes a bit longer depending on the connections underneath.
