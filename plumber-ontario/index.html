Ontario sits directly south of Upland, and Euclid Avenue ties the two cities together. The same 200-foot-wide boulevard the Chaffey brothers laid out in 1882 runs from Upland's Historic Downtown straight into Ontario's, so a technician from our Upland base reaches most of north and central Ontario quickly.

The first thing we ask on an Ontario call is what kind of building it is. A 1920s home near El Morado Court, a newer house in Ontario Ranch, and a restaurant near the airport have almost nothing in common underground, and that decides whether we are looking at original cast iron or newer materials.

The Euclid Avenue Corridor

Historic Model Colony Neighborhoods

The oldest blocks of the Model Colony sit on and around Euclid Avenue, including the El Morado Court Historic District, a narrow street from 1922 designed for Model Ts and horse-drawn buggies, and the older streets near Central School. Homes of that age commonly still have original cast iron or clay drain lines and galvanized supply pipe, and they were never sized for modern fixture counts.

The street trees along the Euclid Avenue corridor, including the pepper trees the city is known for, can also push roots into aging sewer laterals. A drain that keeps backing up in this part of town deserves a camera inspection before another snaking visit, and that is where our sewer line repair work starts.

Southern Ontario

Ontario Ranch And The Newer Neighborhoods

The southern part of the city, planned as the New Model Colony and now called Ontario Ranch, is where most of Ontario's recent home building has happened. Calls from those homes are mostly installs and early-failure parts, such as a water heater replacement, a softener hookup, or a builder-grade fitting that gave out sooner than it should.

Because the homes are new, a problem in the first few years is often a defect or an installation issue rather than wear. We document what we find, which helps if a builder warranty turns out to be involved.

Airport And Commercial

Ontario Mills, The Airport, And The Warehouse Districts

Ontario International Airport, the Ontario Mills outlet center, and the hotels, restaurants, and distribution buildings around them make this one of the busiest commercial plumbing markets in our coverage area. Those properties have their own needs: grease-heavy drain lines, restroom groups that run all day, and backflow assemblies that need annual testing.

Our commercial plumbing and backflow testing work covers those properties, and property managers can set up a standing schedule instead of calling only when something fails. All work in Ontario follows the California Plumbing Code (UPC).
