Water filtration and water softening solve different problems: a softener addresses hardness minerals that damage pipes and appliances, while filtration targets taste, odor, chlorine, and other contaminants in your drinking water. Many households benefit from both, but they're not interchangeable.

We install both whole-home systems that treat all water entering the house and point-of-use systems like under-sink or countertop filters focused specifically on drinking and cooking water.

The Cause, Explained

What's Actually In Upland's Water Supply

Municipal water in Upland, like most California cities, is treated with chlorine or chloramine for disinfection, which is safe but affects taste and odor for many households. This is the most common reason people look into filtration in the first place.

Beyond taste, some households have specific concerns about additional constituents in the water supply, which is worth testing for directly rather than assuming any single filter type addresses everything.

A whole-home system treats water at the point it enters the house, covering everything from drinking water to shower and laundry water, while a point-of-use system focuses narrowly on the kitchen tap or a single fixture.

Households on well water or with older galvanized plumbing sometimes have additional sediment or particulate concerns beyond what municipal customers typically deal with, which changes the filtration approach we'd recommend.

Filtration Vs Softening

Do You Need One, Or Both

A softener and a filtration system solve different problems, and pairing them is common for households dealing with both hard water and taste or odor concerns. If budget is a factor, we can help prioritize based on what's actually bothering you most about the water.

A household mainly bothered by chlorine taste in drinking water is usually well served by a under-sink or reverse osmosis system alone. A household also dealing with scale buildup on fixtures and a water heater failing early benefits more from adding a softener to the mix.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do I need a whole-home system or just an under-sink filter?

Depends on your priorities. Under-sink filters address drinking and cooking water only. Whole-home systems treat everything, including shower and laundry water.

Will filtration reduce my water pressure?

A properly sized system shouldn't cause a noticeable pressure drop. We size systems to your household's actual flow needs.

Is reverse osmosis necessary?

Not for everyone. It's effective for specific concerns but removes beneficial minerals too. We can help determine if it fits your situation.

How often do filters need replacing?

Depends on the system and household usage, typically every six months to a year for most residential filters.

Does filtration remove hard water minerals too?

Standard filtration targets taste, odor, and chlorine, not hardness minerals. For hard water, a softener is the correct tool, and the two systems work well installed together.
