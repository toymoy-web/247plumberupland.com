Tankless water heaters last longer than tank units, but they are far more sensitive to Upland's hard water. Mineral scale builds up on the heat exchanger inside the unit, and once it does, the unit has to work harder to hit the same temperature.

If your tankless unit is a few years old and either throwing an error code or just not delivering the hot water it used to, the fix is usually a flush and descale rather than a new unit. We diagnose the actual cause first and explain what a service visit will and won't fix.

The Cause, Explained

Why Tankless Units Need Regular Service Here

A tank water heater tolerates hard water reasonably well because the sediment simply settles at the bottom. A tankless unit runs water through a narrow heat exchanger, and that same mineral content coats the inside of the exchanger instead of settling anywhere harmless.

Manufacturers generally recommend an annual descale in a hard water area like Upland, compared to every two to three years in a soft water market. Skipping it does not damage the unit right away, but it does shorten its working life and increase the energy it takes to heat the same amount of water.

We hear this pattern often on service calls: a unit installed three or four years ago that has never been flushed, now showing reduced flow or an error code the homeowner assumed meant the unit was failing outright.

Signs To Watch For

Signs Your Tankless Unit Needs Service

Error Code On Display

Most tankless brands show a specific code for scale buildup, ignition failure, or flow sensor issues. We read the code before quoting anything.

Fluctuating Water Temperature

Often scale-related, as the unit struggles to maintain a consistent heat exchange.

Reduced Hot Water Flow

A partially scaled heat exchanger restricts flow even when the burner is working fine.

Unit Is Over 12 Months Since Last Descale

Tank Vs Tankless

Is A Tankless Upgrade Worth It In Upland

Homeowners replacing an aging tank sometimes ask whether switching to tankless makes sense given the local water. It generally still does for the endless hot water and smaller footprint, provided the annual descale is actually kept up.

The honest tradeoff: a tankless unit costs more upfront and needs that yearly service in a hard water market like this one. A tank unit is simpler but has a shorter overall lifespan here regardless of maintenance. Neither is a wrong choice, it depends on how much you value on-demand hot water versus lower upfront cost.

Maintenance

What To Document Before Calling

If your unit is showing an error code, noting exactly what's on the display saves diagnosis time on the visit.

Before you call

  • Write down the exact error code shown on the display, if any
  • Note when the unit was last serviced or descaled, if known
  • Check whether the issue is reduced flow, no hot water at all, or fluctuating temperature
  • Confirm the unit's brand and approximate installation date if available

FAQs

Common Questions

How often should a tankless water heater be descaled in Upland?

Roughly once a year, given the local hard water. Some manufacturers specify this in the warranty terms.

What does an error code on my tankless unit mean?

It depends on the brand and code, but scale buildup, ignition issues, and flow sensor faults are the most common causes we see locally.

Can I descale a tankless unit myself?

It's possible with a pump kit and isolation valves already installed, but a professional flush also includes an inspection that catches early ignition or venting issues before they become bigger repairs.

Is it better to repair or replace an aging tankless unit?

If the unit is under 10 years old and the issue is scale or a single part, repair is usually the better value. Tankless units generally last 15-20 years with regular service.
