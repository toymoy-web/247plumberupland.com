Backflow Testing & Prevention in Upland, CA

Backflow prevention devices protect your drinking water from contamination, and most irrigation systems and commercial properties in Upland require annual certified testing to stay compliant.

A backflow prevention device stops contaminated water from an irrigation system, pool, or commercial process from flowing backward into the clean municipal water supply. Most properties with an irrigation system, and virtually all commercial properties, are required to have one tested annually.

We provide certified testing, new device installation, and repair for failed units, along with the compliance documentation typically required by the water utility or local jurisdiction.

The Cause, Explained

Why Backflow Prevention Matters

A drop in municipal water pressure, which can happen during a water main break or heavy simultaneous demand, can allow water to flow backward through an unprotected connection, pulling contaminants from irrigation systems or industrial processes into the clean water supply.

This is exactly what backflow prevention devices are designed to stop, and it's why testing them annually matters even when there's no visible sign of a problem, since a device can fail internally without any obvious symptom.

California plumbing code requires backflow prevention on connections that present a contamination risk, which is why most irrigation systems, fire sprinkler connections, and commercial process water lines have a device installed as a condition of the original permit.

A device typically installed a decade or more ago may also no longer meet the specific model requirements some water providers now list as approved, which is worth checking during a routine test rather than assuming an older device is automatically fine.

Compliance

Who Needs Annual Testing

Properties with irrigation systems, pools, commercial kitchens, and certain other connections are generally required to have backflow devices tested annually under California plumbing and health code requirements. Requirements can vary by water provider, so we confirm what applies to your specific property and file accordingly.

A missed annual test can result in a notice from your water provider or, in some cases, service restrictions until the device is tested and certified again, so it's worth tracking the due date rather than waiting for a reminder.

What to check before your test is due

  • Confirm the device is visible and accessible, not buried under landscaping or debris
  • Note any visible corrosion, cracking, or continuous water discharge from the device
  • Locate your last test certificate so we can confirm the device model and history
  • Check with your water provider or HOA for the exact date your certification expires

FAQs

Common Questions

Do I really need this tested every year?

In most cases yes, if you have an irrigation system or certain commercial connections. Requirements are set by your water provider and local jurisdiction.

What happens if my device fails the test?

It needs repair or replacement before it can be recertified. We can typically handle this on the same visit if parts are on hand.

Do you file the paperwork with the city or water district?

Yes, we submit the required compliance documentation once testing is complete.

How long does testing take?

Most residential tests take under an hour. Commercial properties with multiple devices take longer.

What happens if I skip a year of testing?

Your water provider may send a notice or, in some cases, restrict service until the device is tested and certified. It's best to stay current on the schedule and keep records handy.
