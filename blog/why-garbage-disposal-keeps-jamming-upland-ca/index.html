Why Your Garbage Disposal Keeps Jamming In Upland, CA

A disposal that jams once in a while is completely normal. One that jams constantly, even with careful use, usually points to a specific, fixable underlying cause.

A disposal jamming occasionally isn't unusual, a stray utensil or an unusually fibrous piece of food can catch anyone off guard. But a disposal that jams repeatedly, even when you're being careful about what goes down it, is a different situation entirely with a handful of likely, identifiable explanations worth ruling out one at a time.

The Most Common Culprits

Fibrous vegetables like celery, corn husks, and onion skins are frequent offenders, since their stringy fibers tend to wrap around the blades rather than being cut cleanly. Coffee grounds and eggshells, despite common belief that they help sharpen blades, actually contribute to buildup that can jam smaller units over time.

Grease and cooking oil are another major factor, not because they jam the blades directly, but because they solidify inside the disposal and drain line as they cool, gradually narrowing the space and making the unit work harder against increased resistance until it eventually stalls.

Foreign objects are a less common but real cause too. Small utensils, bottle caps, or produce stickers that fall in unnoticed can jam a disposal completely regardless of the unit's horsepower, which is why checking the chamber visually is always worth doing before assuming the cause is food-related.

Starchy foods like pasta and rice can also cause problems, though not through jamming exactly, they expand when they absorb residual water in the drain line afterward, which can contribute to a slow-draining sink even when the disposal itself is running fine.

Is Your Disposal Just Undersized

Horsepower matters more than most people realize. A basic 1/3 horsepower disposal, common in older installations and budget builder-grade setups, is genuinely more prone to jamming under normal household use than a 3/4 or 1 horsepower unit, regardless of how carefully you use it.

If your disposal is original to an older home and jams even with reasonable care, the unit itself may simply be undersized for how your household actually uses the kitchen today, especially if cooking habits have changed since the unit was originally installed.

A quick way to check your current unit's horsepower is to look for a label on the side or bottom of the unit itself, usually printed near the model number. If you can't find it, a technician can identify it quickly during a service visit.

How To Safely Clear A Jam Yourself

With the power off at the switch, most disposals have a hex-key opening at the bottom center where a manual crank tool, often included with the unit, can be inserted to manually rotate the blades and work through the obstruction. Never put your hand inside the disposal, even with power off, use the crank tool or a long pair of tongs.

If the unit hums but won't spin even after manually rotating the blades, check the reset button on the bottom of the unit, a small red button that trips when the motor overheats. Pressing it can restore function if that's the actual cause rather than a physical jam.

If neither the crank tool nor the reset button resolves the issue, and the unit still won't run, that's usually a sign the problem is electrical or mechanical rather than a simple jam. It's worth having a technician take a look rather than continuing to force the issue.

Any disposal repair or replacement follows the California Plumbing Code (UPC) requirements for drain and trap connections at the sink.

When It's Time To Stop Repairing And Replace

If jamming is happening weekly or more often despite careful use, and the unit is more than 8 to 10 years old, it's often more practical to replace it with a higher horsepower model than to keep clearing jams indefinitely. The cost of a service call to clear a stuck disposal repeatedly over a year can approach what a proper replacement would have cost outright.

A leak developing at the base of an aging unit is another sign worth combining with jamming frequency when deciding whether repair or replacement makes more sense.

It's also worth considering household changes since the original installation. A disposal that worked fine for a smaller household or lighter cooking habits may simply be undersized now if the household has grown or cooking frequency has increased significantly.

Replacing a disposal is generally a same-day job once the decision is made, which means the actual downside of moving to replacement sooner rather than continuing to nurse an undersized unit along is smaller than most homeowners expect.

What to do before you call about a jammed disposal

  • Turn off power to the disposal at the wall switch before doing anything else
  • Check for the reset button on the bottom of the unit and press it once
  • Use the hex-key crank tool, if available, to manually rotate the blades
  • Never insert your hand into the disposal, even with the power confirmed off
  • Check the disposal's label for its horsepower rating if jamming is frequent

FAQs

Is it bad to run my disposal with cold water instead of hot?

Cold water is actually preferred while running a disposal, since it helps solidify grease into small pieces that pass through more easily rather than melting and coating the blades and line.

Can ice cubes help clean the disposal?

Running ice cubes through periodically can help dislodge minor buildup on the blades, though it won't fix an underlying jamming problem caused by an undersized unit or a specific recurring obstruction.

Why does my disposal smell bad even when it's not jamming?

Trapped food particles breaking down inside the unit is the usual cause. Running cold water with a small amount of dish soap, or occasionally grinding citrus peel, can help with odor between service visits.

Should I try a bigger disposal even if my current one technically still works?

If jamming is frequent enough to be a regular annoyance, upgrading to a higher horsepower unit is often worth it even before the current one fully fails, especially if household cooking habits have grown since the original installation.

Is it normal for a disposal to take longer to grind certain foods?

Yes, fibrous or tough items naturally take a bit longer than soft food scraps. It's only a concern if the unit stalls completely or the delay has gotten noticeably worse over time compared to how it used to perform.

If my kitchen drain backs up in Upland, is the City responsible?

Per the City's sewer backup procedure, Public Works maintains the City sewer mains and property owners maintain the private lateral. Call a plumber first to find the cause.
