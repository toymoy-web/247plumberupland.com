Outdoor Water

Irrigation, Water Lines And Hidden Leaks

Water that escapes through a hidden leak can show up as a high bill before it shows up as a wet spot. If the bill jumps or a patch of lawn stays soggy, leak detection finds the source without trial digging.

Outdoor supply lines, hose bibs and irrigation connections are exposed to weather and landscaping work. A cracked fitting, a line nicked during digging or a failed valve are typical outdoor causes. Repairs range from a single fitting to a section of water line replacement, and we explain which applies.

Sprinkler systems tied into a home's water supply typically use a backflow assembly to protect drinking water. Many water providers require periodic testing, and the rules vary, so check your notice or ask your provider. Our backflow testing team can test and repair assemblies.

If a property has a pool, leaks around pumps, filters and underground lines are a separate call from household leaks. See our pool leak detection page for how that works. Indoor calls follow the usual pattern of clogs, fixtures and water heater replacement.

Quick Checks

Signs Of An Outdoor Leak

Watch The Water Bill

A bill that rises with no change in use is an early sign of a leak. Compare recent bills before you call.

Check The Meter

With every fixture off, a moving meter can point to a leak somewhere past it. Tell us what you saw.

Mind The Sprinklers

Soggy ground near a sprinkler line or a valve box that stays wet deserves a closer look.

FAQs

Upland Hills Country Club Plumbing Questions

Where is the Upland Hills Country Club area?

A state environmental record places the specific plan at 16th Street and North Upland Hills Drive. If you are unsure whether your address is in the area, call and we will help with the plumbing question either way.

How can I tell if I have a leak in my yard?

Look for a water bill that rises without a change in use, soggy ground, or a meter that keeps moving with all fixtures off. A leak detection visit locates the leak before any digging starts.

Does my sprinkler system need backflow testing?

Many water providers require periodic testing of backflow assemblies, and rules vary. Check your notice or ask your provider, and we can test and repair the assembly.

Do you work on condominiums with shared plumbing?

Yes, though shared lines can involve the association. We document where a problem starts so the right party can decide what happens next.

Do you answer emergency calls near the club?

Yes. Our line is answered 24/7. Close the main water valve if you can reach it safely, then call.
