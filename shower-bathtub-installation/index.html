A dripping shower valve or a bathtub that won't hold water at the drain is usually a straightforward repair. A full installation, whether it's a new tub, a walk-in shower, or converting a tub to a shower, is a bigger scope that we plan out before any demolition starts.

We work directly with homeowners on standalone plumbing fixture swaps, and we coordinate with general contractors when the shower or tub work is part of a larger bathroom remodel.

The Cause, Explained

Common Shower And Tub Issues We See

A shower valve that drips or is hard to adjust to temperature is often a worn cartridge, the same underlying cause as a dripping faucet, just inside the wall rather than at a sink.

Older tubs and showers in Upland homes built before the 1990s sometimes have outdated single-handle valves without modern anti-scald protection, which is worth discussing during any repair or remodel conversation.

A bathtub or shower pan that flexes noticeably underfoot usually points to a subfloor issue rather than the fixture itself, and it's worth checking before installing a new unit on top of a compromised base.

Signs To Watch For

Signs You Need Repair Or A New Installation

Valve Dripping Or Hard To Adjust

Usually a worn cartridge, a straightforward repair without needing tile work.

Water Pooling At The Drain

Can mean a failed drain seal or a slow slope issue in an older tub.

Visible Cracks In The Tub Or Shower Pan

Not repairable long-term. Replacement is the safer option.

Wanting A Tub-To-Shower Conversion

A common request for households prioritizing accessibility or a more modern look.

Conversions

Tub-To-Shower Conversions

Converting an underused tub to a walk-in shower is one of the most requested upgrades we see, often for accessibility reasons or simply to modernize an older bathroom. We handle the plumbing relocation and valve work, coordinating with a tile or surround installer for the finished look.

A conversion typically involves relocating the drain, adjusting supply line height for the new valve position, and confirming the existing waterproofing membrane extends properly for a shower rather than a tub, which is worth planning before any demolition starts.

FAQs

Common Questions

Can you convert my tub to a walk-in shower?

Yes, we handle the plumbing relocation and valve work for conversions, coordinating with a tile or surround installer for the finished surfaces.

Is a dripping shower valve an easy fix?

In most cases yes, it's typically a worn cartridge replacement without needing to open up tile or wall surfaces.

Do you work with my general contractor on a full remodel?

Yes, we regularly coordinate directly with contractors on the plumbing scope of larger bathroom remodels.

Do new installations include anti-scald protection?

Yes, current code requires anti-scald valve protection on new shower and tub installations, which we include as standard.

How do I know if my subfloor can support a new tub or shower pan?

We check for flexing or soft spots before installation. A compromised subfloor needs repair first, otherwise even a new fixture will develop problems.
