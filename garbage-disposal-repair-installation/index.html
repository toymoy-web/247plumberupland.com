A disposal that hums but doesn't spin is almost always jammed, not broken, and clearing it is quick once we're on site with the right tools. A disposal that's completely silent when switched on is more often an electrical or reset issue than a motor failure.

We also handle full replacement when a unit has failed for good or a kitchen renovation calls for an upgrade to a higher horsepower model.

The Cause, Explained

Why Disposals Jam Or Fail In Upland Kitchens

Fibrous foods, coffee grounds, and grease are the most common jam and clog causes we see, especially in households that treat the disposal as a general trash disposal rather than for small food scraps.

A leak at the base of the unit usually means a failed seal, which happens naturally over years of use and isn't a sign of misuse. This is a straightforward repair or replacement depending on the unit's age.

Older units, especially those installed more than eight to ten years ago, run at lower horsepower than current models and jam more easily under normal household use, which is worth factoring into a repair-versus-replace decision.

Cold water run alongside the disposal during use also helps solidify grease before it reaches the trap, reducing how often the unit jams in the first place. All installations follow the California Plumbing Code (UPC) for drain and trap connections.

Signs To Watch For

Signs Your Disposal Needs Attention

Humming But Not Spinning

Usually a jam. Often fixable without replacing the unit.

No Sound At All

Check the reset button first. If that doesn't help, it may be an electrical issue.

Leaking At The Base

Typically a failed seal, needing repair or replacement depending on age.

Unusual Grinding Noise

Can mean something solid is caught in the blades, worth checking before it damages the motor.

Installation

Upgrading To A New Disposal

If your current unit is undersized for the household or has failed beyond repair, we install new units with higher horsepower and quieter operation, properly connected to both the drain line and dishwasher if applicable.

A common upgrade request is moving from a basic 1/3 horsepower unit to a 3/4 or 1 horsepower model, which handles tougher scraps and jams far less often for a household that cooks regularly.

FAQs

Common Questions

My disposal just hums, is it broken?

Usually not. A hum without spinning almost always means a jam, which we can typically clear on the same visit.

Why does my disposal leak from the bottom?

Most often a worn seal from normal use over time. Depending on the unit's age, we repair or recommend replacement.

Can you install a disposal where there wasn't one before?

Yes, provided the sink and drain setup can accommodate it. We assess this during the visit.

What shouldn't go down a disposal?

Fibrous vegetables, coffee grounds, grease, and bones are the most common causes of jams and premature wear.

Is it worth upgrading to a higher horsepower unit?

For a household that cooks often, yes. A higher horsepower disposal jams less and handles tougher food waste with less strain on the motor.

How long does installation take?

A straightforward swap of an existing unit usually takes under an hour. A first-time installation can take longer depending on the sink setup.
