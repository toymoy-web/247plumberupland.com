Most sewer line problems in Upland show up as recurring backups, slow drainage across multiple fixtures, or a sewage odor in the yard. Before quoting any repair, we run a camera through the line to see exactly what's happening and where.

A localized crack or a single joint with root intrusion is a different, much cheaper repair than a fully collapsed line. Camera verification is what tells us which one we're actually dealing with, rather than guessing based on symptoms alone.

The Cause, Explained

What Causes Sewer Line Problems In Upland

Root intrusion is the most common cause we see, particularly on older clay or cast iron lines near mature trees close to downtown Upland and along the historic Euclid Avenue corridor. Roots enter through small cracks or loose joints and expand over time.

Ground shifting from the area's clay soil composition can also stress older sewer lines, especially on hillside properties where the line runs at a steeper grade. This shows up as a localized crack rather than root intrusion.

Repairs follow the California Plumbing Code (the state's adopted Uniform Plumbing Code, or UPC), which governs acceptable pipe materials and joint methods for sewer line work.

Signs To Watch For

Signs Of A Sewer Line Problem

Recurring Backups

Especially if snaking clears it temporarily but it returns within weeks.

Multiple Slow Drains At Once

Points to a main line issue rather than a single fixture problem.

Sewage Odor In The Yard

Can indicate a crack or break allowing seepage before it reaches the street.

Unusually Green Or Lush Grass Patch

Roots feeding off a nearby leak sometimes show up this way first.

Repair Options

Spot Repair Vs Trenchless Lining Vs Excavation

Spot Repair

  • For a single localized crack or joint
  • Least disruptive, fastest turnaround
  • Lower cost when the issue is isolated
  • Not suitable for widespread damage

Trenchless Lining

  • For root intrusion along a longer section
  • No full excavation required
  • Higher upfront cost than spot repair
  • Not suitable for a fully collapsed line
When Excavation Is Necessary

If The Line Is Fully Collapsed

A fully collapsed or severely offset section of pipe can't be lined or spot-repaired and needs traditional excavation. This is less common than root intrusion or a localized crack, but the camera inspection tells us definitively which situation we're in before any digging starts.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do you always have to dig up the yard?

No. Spot repair and trenchless lining can often fix the problem with minimal or no excavation, depending on what the camera inspection finds.

How do you know it's roots and not something else?

The camera inspection shows this directly. Root intrusion has a distinct visual signature different from a crack, offset joint, or collapse.

Will the problem come back after a spot repair?

A properly executed spot repair addresses the specific defect and typically holds long-term, though nearby sections of an aging line can develop separate issues later.

Is sewer line repair covered by insurance?

Usually not for gradual causes like root intrusion, though a sudden collapse from an external event may be treated differently. Check your specific policy.
